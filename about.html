Being Straight About the Title

My title says operations, and I'm not going to dress that up. The day-to-day is execution: taking live plans, turning them into configurations, getting them validated, and staying on them after they go out. It's real work on a real game and I'm good at it.

But the part I can't switch off is building. When something in our process is slow or manual, I go and write the tool. When I have an idea for a game, I build it rather than write it down. That's the direction I'm moving, closer to owning the product and game calls, not just making them happen.

  • What the job gives me: a live game at scale, real launches, real players, and four teams to work across
  • What I bring to it: the tooling that makes the repetitive parts stop being repetitive
  • Where I'm heading: owning more of what gets built, not just how reliably it ships

How I Work

  • Notice what's annoying: something's slow, confusing, or takes four manual steps it shouldn't. I go find out why.
  • Work out how it really runs: the loop, the economy, the data model, who touches what, before I touch anything visible.
  • Build the smallest real version: not a doc, not a deck. Something you can click through or play.
  • Get it in front of people: ship it, watch what actually happens, fix what's wrong.
  • Then make it boring: reliable, repeatable, and safe to run again next week. That part matters most on a live game.
