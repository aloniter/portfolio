The Flow, Screen by Screen

The Argument That Started It

Every week, same thing. A long WhatsApp thread, twenty-one names in no particular order, and then someone tries to split them into three teams while everyone else explains why that's unfair. It took twenty minutes and somebody always walked onto the pitch annoyed. The game hadn't even started.

Fair Isn't the Same as Balanced

Balancing teams by algorithm is the easy version, and it doesn't work. If a program hands you three teams, everyone just argues with the program instead. People had to watch it happen and agree it was fair while it was happening. It also had to survive a pitch-side group on bad phone signal, run by whoever's organising that week, who is not a developer and will not debug anything.

How a Draft Night Goes

1. Paste the thread

Copy the WhatsApp messages straight in. It pulls out the names and drops the duplicates and the chat noise.

2. Pick three captains

Three captains, one each for Red, White and Black. Decided before anyone sees the player pool.

3. Draft, snaking

Pick order flips every round, so going last isn't a punishment. Everyone can see whose turn it is.

4. Check, then send

The host gets one look at the finished teams before they go out. Last chance to catch a mistake.

Where I Landed on the Awkward Bits

A draft everyone watches, not a clever algorithm

A snake draft is something everyone already understands from watching sport. An optimiser is something you have to trust. What it costs: the teams aren't mathematically perfect. Nobody has ever cared, because they watched it happen and picked their own players. That was always the actual problem.

Saving every pick as it happens

Draft state is stored locally, so a dropped connection or an accidental refresh doesn't wipe the night. What it costs: a lot more state handling behind something that looks trivially simple, but losing fifteen minutes of picks right before kickoff would have killed the app immediately.

One last look before it goes out

The host confirms the teams before anyone else sees them. What it costs: an extra tap in a flow I'd otherwise want to keep tight. Worth it, because once those teams hit the group chat they're real.

What I'd Tell Myself

  • It was a rules problem, not an interface problem: once the rules felt fair, the screens were easy
  • Next up: proper reconnect handling, and letting someone else take over as host mid-draft
  • Next up: optional player ratings that suggest balance without taking the picks away from people
  • Next up: match history and a league table, since it is the same group every week anyway
