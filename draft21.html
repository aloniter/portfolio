Everyone watches the picks happen

Problem

Every week, the same thing. A long WhatsApp thread, twenty-one names in no particular order, and then someone tries to split them into three teams while everyone else explains why that's unfair. It took twenty minutes and somebody always walked onto the pitch annoyed. The game hadn't even started.

Fair isn't the same as balanced. Balancing teams by algorithm is the easy version and it doesn't work. If a program hands you three teams, everyone just argues with the program instead. People had to watch it happen and agree it was fair while it was happening. It also had to survive a pitch-side group on bad signal, run by whoever's organising that week, who is not a developer and will not debug anything.

The flow

Decisions

A draft everyone watches, not a clever algorithm

A snake draft is something everyone already understands from watching sport. An optimiser is something you have to trust. What it costs: the teams aren't mathematically perfect. Nobody has ever cared, because they watched it happen and picked their own players. That was always the actual problem.

Saving every pick as it happens

Draft state is stored locally, so a dropped connection or an accidental refresh doesn't wipe the night. What it costs: a lot more state handling behind something that looks trivially simple. Losing fifteen minutes of picks right before kickoff would have killed the app immediately.

One last look before it goes out

The host confirms the teams before anyone else sees them. What it costs: an extra tap in a flow I'd otherwise keep tight. Worth it: once those teams hit the group chat they're real.

Build

  • The whole host flow, from pasted names to teams in the group chat
  • Snake draft logic: three teams, seven each, order flipping every round
  • Captain setup, the review step, and autosave that survives a bad connection
  • Shipped as a PWA so nobody has to install anything

Result

  • Weekly Still in use, every game night
  • ~20 min Of arguing it replaced
  • 21 Players sorted into three teams of seven

It runs across everyone's phones and my group still uses it every week. It's a small thing, but it's a complete one: I saw a problem in my own life, built the fix, and people kept using it without being asked to. It was a rules problem, not an interface problem. Once the rules felt fair, the screens were easy.
