A real finding, circled on site

Problem

An inspector walks a building and comes back with a hundred photos and a page of notes. Then the real work starts: sitting down that evening, matching every photo to what was wrong with it, and typing it all into a document. Hours per job, every job — and the longer the gap between seeing the problem and writing it down, the easier it is to miss one.

This looks like an app-design job and isn't. It's a data problem wearing a UI. Photos, annotations and findings have to stay tied together while someone moves fast through a site with a phone in one hand, and the numbered list at the end has to come out right every time without anyone retyping it. Get the structure wrong and the export quietly produces a broken report — one that goes to a paying client.

The app

Decisions

Photos first, forms never

Inspectors work with a camera, not a keyboard. The app opens on the camera rather than on a form waiting to be filled in. What it costs — all the complexity moves into annotating and organising afterwards, and that part has to be quick enough that nobody's standing in a stairwell waiting for it.

The findings list builds itself

Mark a photo and it becomes a numbered finding automatically. There is no separate step where you write the list again. That's the entire reason things stopped getting missed. What it costs — the data model had to be right on day one, because the whole report is generated from it. Get it wrong later and you're rebuilding the foundation.

Export to Word and PDF, boring as that is

I could have built a much nicer report viewer inside the app. Nobody wanted one. They wanted the file they already send to clients: Word when someone needs to edit it, PDF when it's final. What it costs — two real export pipelines to build and keep working, instead of one pretty screen. That's the line between something people demo and something people use.

Build

  • Scope: decided what went in the first version and, harder, what didn't
  • All of it: camera, annotation, data layer, export pipeline — in Swift, solo
  • Structure: the Projects → Photos → Findings model the whole report is generated from
  • Shipping: through App Store review, and kept working once people depended on it

Result

  • 108 Open findings in one real report, exported in a single pass
  • On the App Store Shipped, reviewed, and in daily use
  • Evening gone The write-up that used to follow every job

That 108 is from an actual job. At that volume the old way was an entire evening of typing, and every one of those lines was a chance to miss something. It's on the App Store, and a family-run inspection company uses it for real client work.

It's also the clearest proof I have that this isn't only a gaming habit. Same instinct, different industry, and it still ended in something people actually use.
