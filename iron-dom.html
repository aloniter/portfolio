Iron Dom

Mobile and browser arcade defense prototype built around quick reactions, interception timing, and escalating wave pressure.

Role Solo Designer & Developer
Timeline Portfolio prototype
Platform Mobile / Browser
Focus Reaction controls and wave pressure
Play Live Demo

Screens

Product Goal

Turn a simple defensive mechanic into a compact arcade game that feels clear, fast, and playable on mobile. The player protects the city by reacting quickly, intercepting incoming threats, and surviving escalating waves.

My Role

  • Designed the core action-defense loop from concept to playable prototype
  • Built the browser game flow, player input, collision logic, and scoring feedback
  • Tuned wave pacing so the pressure increases without making the screen unreadable
  • Shaped the visual feedback around clear threat, action, and result moments

Problem / Opportunity

I wanted a small prototype that could prove a game does not need a complicated rule set to feel tense. The opportunity was to make every second readable: see the threat, respond quickly, and immediately understand whether the city is safer.

What I Defined

  • Arcade defense loop built around scanning, targeting, interception, and survival
  • Incoming threat behavior that creates urgency without overwhelming the player instantly
  • Reaction-based controls that work for quick mobile play sessions
  • Wave escalation rules that add pressure through pacing and threat density
  • Visual feedback for launches, interceptions, scoring, and city damage

How I Worked with Engineering / QA

This was a solo prototype, so I treated the build like a small implementation handoff to myself. I broke the game into clear states, tested edge cases around missed threats and collisions, and kept the feedback readable enough to validate on both desktop and mobile screens.

Metrics or Success Criteria

  • Players can understand the objective within the first few seconds
  • Incoming threats remain readable as waves become faster and denser
  • Interceptions give immediate visual confirmation and scoring feedback
  • Mobile controls feel responsive during short, repeated play sessions
  • The prototype communicates a complete game loop without extra explanation

Iteration / What Changed

The strongest version came from simplifying the interaction and making the feedback faster. Instead of adding more systems, I focused on clean threat readability, clear interception moments, and a pressure curve that ramps up while the goal stays obvious.

Next Iterations

  • Add a small set of threat types that change prioritization without adding complexity
  • Introduce combo or accuracy bonuses to reward strong interception streaks
  • Track wave survival, hit rate, and missed threats to guide future tuning
  • Expand mobile polish with stronger start, pause, and restart states
